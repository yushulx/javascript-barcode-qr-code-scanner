Scope

This generator covers every jurisdiction AAMVA assigns an Issuer Identification Number to: 50 US states, the District of Columbia, 5 US territories, 13 Canadian provinces and territories, and 2 Mexican states — 71 jurisdictions in total. All of them use the AAMVA PDF417 format, so the scanner parses them with the AAMVA_DL_ID code spec.

Driver licenses from other countries (EU/EEA, UK, Australia, India, Japan, Brazil, …) are not AAMVA documents. They use national or regional formats that the AAMVA parser does not map, so a scanner restricted to AAMVA_DL_ID will read the barcode but not resolve the fields.

South Africa is a different case, and it cannot be generated at all. The PDF417 on the back of a South African driving license is not plaintext. Its 720 bytes open with a 4-byte version header, then split into six blocks (5 × 128 bytes + 1 × 74 bytes) that are RSA-encrypted with a private key held by the issuing authority; a reader reverses it with the published public key. Anyone can decode a genuine card, nobody can mint one without that private key — the encryption is precisely the anti-forgery mechanism. Dynamsoft's SOUTH_AFRICA_DL spec implements that decryption step, which is why it needs a real card as input rather than a crafted one.

That contrast is the useful takeaway for testing: an AAMVA barcode is plaintext, so a parseable one proves nothing about authenticity — it only proves the payload is well-formed. The South African format is signed, so a parseable one does prove origin. Neither replaces a document check.

License-number patterns, addresses and vehicle classes are plausible samples rather than an authoritative per-jurisdiction specification. The purpose of this page is to exercise a scanner end to end, not to reproduce any particular issuing authority's output.
