What this covers

  • Merchant-presented mode. The merchant shows the QR and the customer scans it. This is the mode behind Pix, PromptPay, UPI, SGQR, DuitNow and most national schemes.
  • CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE. Computed over the payload including the literal 6304 that introduces it, but not the four hex characters it protects. Getting that order wrong yields a code that looks perfect and is rejected everywhere.
  • Ascending tag order. The specification requires top-level objects in ascending tag order, so the form does not choose the sequence — the model does.
  • The 99-character limit. TLV lengths are written as two digits, so no single field value can exceed 99 characters. Long merchant names fail here rather than in a wallet.
  • Error-correction level M. What EMVCo specifies for payment QR codes.

To read a real payment QR rather than make one, use the online EMV QR scanner, which parses the TLV payload field by field and checks the checksum.

The payload is assembled and rendered locally with bwip-js; nothing you type is uploaded. The round-trip check runs Dynamsoft Barcode Reader in your browser.
