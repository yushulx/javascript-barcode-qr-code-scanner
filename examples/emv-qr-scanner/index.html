Who actually runs on this format

  • Counter stickers and hawker stalls. A static code printed once and taped to a counter — the customer types the amount. Pix in Brazil, PromptPay in Thailand, UPI in India, SGQR in Singapore and DuitNow in Malaysia are all this same TLV envelope, each with its own merchant account template inside tag 26 to 51.
  • POS screens and checkout apps. A dynamic code minted per transaction carries the amount and a fresh reference, so the payer confirms an exact figure instead of typing one and the merchant knows which sale settled.
  • Invoices and billing. The bill number sub-tag binds a printed QR to one account or invoice — utility bills, school fees, tax payments.
  • Acquirers, PSPs and risk teams. The CRC detects a scanned or photographed code that was altered in transit — the classic attack is swapping the merchant's sticker for a fraudster's. The merchant category code feeds interchange categories and reporting.
  • Developers. A decoded, named field list is what you need to test your own wallet or POS parser, check a CRC implementation, or generate sample payloads for demos and CI — which is what the paired EMV QR generator is for.

How the parse works

  • The checksum runs first. CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE over the payload up to and including the 6304 header. A payload that fails is reported as altered or not an EMV QR at all — its fields are never shown as if trustworthy.
  • Structure before meaning. A payload qualifies as EMV only if it starts with tag 00 carrying value 01. Everything else that decodes as a QR — a URL, a Wi-Fi login, a vCard — is reported for what it is instead of parsed into nonsense.
  • Nested templates are walked, not dumped. Merchant account information (tags 26-51) and the additional data field (62) contain sub-TLVs; the table indents them under their parent.
  • Real-world variation exists. Published scheme examples do not always use the sub-tag numbers the specification names. The scanner shows what is literally present rather than silently renumbering it.
